USE CASE: infographic-diagram. Create one finished, highly readable Simplified Chinese editorial technical explainer, as a clean flat image rather than a photographed mockup.

FIXED VISUAL IDENTITY: Reference image 1 is the permanent visual master. Match its warm ivory background, charcoal copy, indigo primary elements, quiet periwinkle secondary elements and restrained clay-orange accent. The palette and type hierarchy supplied below are fixed. Brand marks may keep their actual colors. Do not replace warm ivory with stark white, gray-blue, black or colored full-bleed backgrounds. No neon, watercolor, retro paper, 3D or cartoon style. A whisper of paper grain (2-4% strength) is welcome; no dot grids, no halftone.

TYPOGRAPHY: Follow the master's elegant modern sans-serif visual language. Main titles are large but regular-to-medium weight, never extra-black poster lettering; the same applies to chapter and lane headings. Section and node headings are medium weight; explanatory text is regular, high contrast and comfortably readable — supporting captions are ink at 70-80% opacity, never light gray on ivory. Small English annotations may be monospace. Keep Simplified Chinese character forms and all quoted labels exact. Do not change the font family aesthetic by topic. These are visual typography targets, not a claim that a raster image embeds an actual font file.

SPACING & AIR: Generous breathing room is part of the style, not wasted space. Respect the supplied spacing values: exterior margins, section gaps, node gaps and a reserved connector corridor around the node graphics. Left-align text blocks; captions never run wider than the declared caption width. Keep a consistent vertical rhythm: every section band shares the same internal spacing. When content feels tight, reduce supporting micro-illustrations first, never the text or the declared relations.

LAYOUT: Obey the selected layout preset and permanent master. Use aligned text, consistent spacing, generous breathing room and fine dividers. Avoid turning the page into a dashboard of equal rounded cards. Minimal pale surfaces are allowed only to group meaningful information. No heavy black frames, thick cartoon outlines, excessive badges, drop shadows or unrelated ornaments. Decorative micro-squares are optional, tiny and subordinate; do not copy the master's RAG name or content into a different topic.

TEXT AND CONTENT: Text carries real explanation. Render all content-plan titles and captions accurately, not as random line placeholders. Any abstract strokes inside a tiny illustrative document or vector glyph are merely a supporting symbol and do not replace the stated caption. The left introduction and each declared section must remain readable. Do not remove explanations to make the page look minimal. Do not invent quotations, statistics, tool choices, guarantees, extra steps or people. Keep the takeaway only once, factual and quiet: no imperative slogans, no exclamation marks. Do not print JSON keys or graph IDs on the image.

TECHNICAL RELATIONS: Every declared node must be visible with its exact title and caption. Every declared connection must have correct endpoints and arrow direction. Connect wrapped flows and cross-section continuations visibly. Process, evidence, alternative and human-feedback arrows must be distinguishable by label and routing. Indigo is the ordinary relation color; clay orange is reserved for the content's declared single most important evidence, decision or transfer relation — at most one orange highlight per image. Do not invent a feedback loop or imply that evaluation automatically trains a model. For comparison or structure diagrams use the declared relationships rather than fabricating an execution sequence.

CONNECTOR LINE CRAFT: Connectors are drawn, not merely present. Give every line a consistent visible-but-light stroke weight across the whole image; keep elbows as soft right angles or single curves, never long unchecked diagonals. Reserve a clear connector corridor around node graphics before composing the page; do not let any stroke run through, under or tangent to text. Place edge labels in the reserved gaps beside their line, where they can be read without zooming. Render each declared directed edge exactly once, with ONE small filled arrowhead only, at its destination edge. Each line must visibly start at its source node and finish at its destination node. Never end a path at a section heading, divider, number badge or floating label. No disconnected horizontal segments or decorative arrows. Route cross-section continuations all the way into the actual next node; keep them separate from feedback routes. A return path has its own clearly labeled direction and must not gain an arrowhead at its source. At crossings, no junction is implied without an explicit branch. For success/error branches, derive the source of each path from the content plan: an error path may not originate at a success-response node, and every response node must have its declared exit. Reduce supporting illustrations to create routing space when needed, not the necessary text or relations.

BRANDS: When additional images are labeled as logos, use their actual recognizable shapes, small beside the corresponding tool name and node. Keep the tool's name visible. Do not replace a software mark with a generic brain, database cylinder or invented icon. Repeated logos should be quiet and consistent in size. If the content contains no branded tool, add none.

COMPOSITION FINISH: Keep all content within generous safe margins, with no clipped labels, overlaps or arrows crossing text. Supporting geometric symbols may explain chunking, a request, a document or a vector but must stay subordinate to the words. Do not reproduce the master's specific examples unless they appear in the current content. Final result: a useful, beautiful, calm technology-magazine explainer with complete information, controlled color, drawn connector lines and consistent typography.
